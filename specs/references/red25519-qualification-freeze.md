### Reference signature-transcript divergence

Executed and classified, not waived:

1. i2pr verifies all ten official Red25519 vectors published in the pinned specification.
2. A control experiment (run during Plan 331, not committed) re-verified the same ten signatures
   with a bare-SHA-512 transcript: 0/10 verify. The specification's domain and length framing are
   therefore required by the published corpus, not optional.
3. The pinned i2pd revision signs type 11 with `SHA-512(T ‖ A ‖ M)` and `SHA-512(R ‖ A ‖ M)` — no
   `I2P_Red25519H(x)` domain, no length framing — and routes type 11 through its plain Ed25519
   verifier. Executed result: the i2pd signature does not verify under the specification equation.
4. Source-level observation: the string `I2P_Red25519H` does not occur anywhere in the pinned Java
   I2P tree, so Java's type-11 engine cannot be applying the specified domain either. Java's
   `RedDSAEngine` mixes an 80-byte nonce and the public key into a SHA-512 digest, matching the
   same bare form.

Normative specification plus its own published vector corpus decide, so i2pr keeps the specified
transcript. The consequence for the branch is real and is carried into the Plan 331 closure
record: a type-11 encrypted LeaseSet2 signed by i2pr will not verify under either pinned reference,
and a type-11 record published by either reference will not verify under i2pr. Blinding, alpha,
credential-facing derivations, and the DHT storage key all agree, so address derivation and lookup
interoperate; only the signature transcript does not.
